This is a brain dump.

I've been practising some NeetCode recently and I realised I have forgotten the feeling of being emotionally attached 
to my code in the way I felt at university, when I coded on paper.

Now with IDE autocomplete and AI help it costs next to nothing to iterate.

But now when I have spent 30 minutes handwriting a
suboptimal solution - I've become very aware of my [sunk-cost-fallacy](https://thedecisionlab.com/biases/the-sunk-cost-fallacy), 
in abandoning progress to try something better. I am glad AI has made this less of a problem now.

However the positive side of sentimentality is, coding becomes an art. I place more effort to produce pretty code. It is
satisfying and I admire others' solutions written at this standard.

I have noticed some development of a velocity addiction across the industry, where when test suites pass - that is
where refinement is complete for a change, even for production environments. I think we all can benefit from some nostalgia 
to be more craftsman-like even if hand writing code is becoming less relevant.

One could argue that prettiness does not always deliver business value and it will mostly be agents reading it anyway, which
may be right in the future - but I'm still shaping my professional opinion.
