## How I graduated at the top of my class in Computer Science

##### <div className="kbd">31st October 2025</div>
##### *10min read*
---

Before reading this I recommend watching Veritasium's video discussing 
[Luck and Survivorship Bias](https://youtu.be/3LopI4YeC4I?si=hkJ65gI2iKdqr7lI).
What I will discuss worked because of who I am, my environment, luck and likely many factors I am not aware of. 
If you are a student, don't take this as gospel.

#### Choosing the right degree
At best I am a mediocre classic academic, at A-Level I finished with a B in Physics and a C in Maths.
The reason I've chosen Computer Science is it almost perfectly avoids my main weaknesses (writing, maths, theory)
and is coursework heavy, rewarding my strengths (practicality, problem solving and thirst for novelty in tech).

It is cliche advice. But I've seen many people try to power through Computer Science when it's the wrong fit because
 it is any of: "Valuable", "High-Paying", "Respectable" degree. 

I've heard some people argue, regardless, it is a matter of discipline and hard work to overcome. Yes you can, but should you? (not a rhetorical question)

Notice how infrequently I have to tap into willpower compared to Maths? I already consume a ton of engineering content on
youtube, listen to podcasts, love coding side projects and almost everything just clicks. The only times I needed to leverage 
discipline is for writing and maths bits of the degree.

Willpower is a limited resource that can be slow to recover and I do everything to minimise how often I'm forced 
to tap into it. If you are already treading water, and a storm comes - you will be the first to sink.
You will burnout faster and have no capacity to perform crunches for higher performance when needed.

However both discipline and willpower can be trained, expanded and important to master but that is different topic. If you are curious I 
recommend reading 
[Can't Hurt Me by David Goggins](https://books.google.nl/books?id=OSchEAAAQBAJ&lpg=PA1&pg=PA1#v=onepage&q&f=false). 
He became a top U.S. Navy SEAL operator while being genetically negatively buoyant and having a heart condition where his
 muscles receive half the oxygen needed, he is a master of discipline.

I am lucky to have Computer Science as a good match, I am not sure what I would do, had I been born 100 years ago.

#### Environment
Some students say they optimise their environment by "going to the library or a cafe for work".

I work best at home, I've built an environment tailored for my workflow, comfort and to minimise strain.
- I've setup two monitors comfortably so I can see more with minimal eye movement and less neck strain.
- Standing desk
- Backlighting, minimises screen/background contrast eye strain
- Comfortable keyboard + mouse
- Dock + KVM to switch my peripherals and monitors to a work laptop with one click
- Dual boot windows and linux (more later)
- Smallest room with high window/room ratio (more later)

I don't see myself ever replicating this in a cafe. 

Also a trip to a cafe as a precondition for work, creates an obstacle to start doing work. If you are curious about making 
good habits I recommend 
[Atomic Habits by James Clear](https://books.google.com/books/about/Atomic_Habits.html?id=fFCjDQAAQBAJ).

During COVID lockdown, psychologist's recommendations were to create rituals for mental work/ life divide when sleeping 
in the same room, could be just putting on a work shirt. My take on this is having dual boot 
partitions for Windows and Linux. When I am in downtime mode, I boot into Windows - it has my games installed, work mode 
boot into Linux - no games.

#### Friends
I've seen students go into solitary or "monk mode", some go as far to uninstall WhatsApp for weeks 
in order to complete coursework.

I could not disagree more with this.

Going into solitary/ "monk mode" can work for a very short period of time, but if you look at my discipline/ willpower graph in this 
environment you would force your intensity line to max out regardless of where your tipping point is. You 
will incur fatigue and train your subconscious to start associating your field of work with solitary. It can also do the 
inverse of making you associate downtime with guilt and evidence for lack of productivity. 

Friends and family are not to be seen as a distraction from study. Psychologically they are key to unwinding, 
ordering thoughts and good friends will even point out blind spots, making work that much better.

The only situations I can think of where cutting off being reasonable, is if you have bad friends and their only idea of going out 
to catch up is getting wasted - in that case they will do more damage than good. Here it is better off being lonely
and going on a search for new and more like minded friends. I am lucky to have good friends from the start of uni.

Chris Williamson who has interviewed many top performers in various fields has made this same 
[observation](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/DF_FkTOAIXw).

#### Memes, Breaks and Downtime
As much as people love to look down on memes - they are exactly what I need throughout my day. When
mentally working through very hard problems and having so much of my life being serious and highly optimised, it is easy to 
become mentally stuck. Spending even just 1 min disconnecting from my challenges, in the opposite domain,
resets me to try to tackle a problem from a different angle. 

There is a running joke that engineers have 10 coffee/tea breaks a day. I usually do 1-2,
but they are also great for stepping away from output and making space for thinking about hard problems. In this domain 
tea thinking time is also working time.

High quality downtime is also important, programming is mentally intense. One way I do this is I time box my work day and
having Saturdays as sacred rest time and Sundays for church and family, I avoid blurring life/ work divide. Even though I 
might technically lose out on extra time spent on problems, the quality when I am back makes up for that and the proof
is in the pudding.

Being able to live with parents without needing a part time job was luck and a privilege, it was one less thing to 
drain my willpower bucket and left more capacity for high quality work. Universities like
 [Cambridge advise capping side work to 6-10hrs](https://www.cambridgestudents.cam.ac.uk/your-course/postgraduate-study/working-while-you-study).


#### Sleep
Learning the science of sleep has been useful. I recommend watching this video 
[The Science & Practice of Perfecting Your Sleep](https://youtu.be/JaRGJVrJBQ8?si=TzFZfRThCPM0M8vO). I am

- you cannot catchup on sleep
- importance of light exposure within 15min of waking
- bedtime rituals + habits
- CONSISTENCY of sleeping + waking timing is as important as TIME sleeping

now I am in love with the sport. Before I started my cortisol (stress hormone) levels were high enough to diagnose
cushing's disease and blood pressure was at 180. After losing weight my hormones normalised, my anxiety dropped and on top 
I now have a physique I feel comfortable in.

Caffeine is an awesome drug for performance! (When used correctly.)
Caffeine half life can vary anywhere from 1.5 to 9.5 hours, experimenting is the only way to find out. For me if I 
have a coffee at lunch it will give me a work boost and last long enough into my gym session on the evening, but not so long it ruins my sleep.
This [Caffeine Simulator Website](https://observablehq.com/@elibryan/caffeine-simulator) is great for figuring out what 
sensible caffeine amount and timing looks like for you.

I do 1 week caffeine breaks for every 3 weeks of caffeine use to prevent tolerance.

Motivation is a weak force to be leveraged but not depended on.

It is much better to rely on discipline, that is the ability to do something regardless of how I wake up, how I feel,
the difficulty of the task. The backwards arrow is inconsistent, some days it might be small, other days it
might be 20x the size.

But if motivation works for me it is a bonus. How did I maximise the two arrows pushing and pulling me forward? 

There is a famous proverb, "Hope for the best, prepare for the worst". Many say it, few live it.

When "Hoping for the best" that is the positive vision, xyz is going to be the result of this - the more detailed this
vision the better. It must be honest too, you would be surprised how many top performers I've looked at and spoken to say 
their motivation is wealth or women. You might say it is shallow, but it is honest and they leveraged that force to overcome great challenges.
Maybe you are a wholesome human being and it is solely creating positive change for the world but be real and 
honest to maximise this. For me it's novelty I want to be at the tip of the spear to have the privilege to work on the most novel projects.

The pushing arrow is fear, anxiety, a negative vision - "preparing for the worst". For me this fear was very real and much 
larger than the pull, with the state of the current engineering job market, just having a 1st class degree is not enough to 
land a Junior SWE job. I need to go beyond and stand out. Computer Science is a field with one of the highest unemployment 
rates right in 2025, there is nothing I would hate more than being stuck in a basic job like retail, living paycheck to paycheck 
and no future development prospects. I have a love for engineering and it would be wasted in this scenario.

I am a neurotic person, I've also learned it has it's positives and if leveraged correctly I can do great things.

The two visions, detailed and grounded in reality, work together to create a strong force that drives me forward.

#### Organisation & Strategy
Leveraging all previous gains and having a lot of extra water in my willpower bucket I applied it to organisation and
strategy.

I had smaller visions too but these were associated with actionable strategy, for modules where there would be multiple
submissions I said to myself two stories:
- Let's get a grade so high on this checkpoint so the next one will be easy and I can achieve a 1st with minimal effort.
- Let's get a grade so high, that on the next checkpoint I will still pass if I submit nothing from an emergency or being ill.

I tracked all of these checkpoints on a spreadsheet, by the time I got to my dissertation project my average was high enough
for me to graduate with a 1st even if I got just a passing grade on my dissertation.

I always rationed my work into small pieces to do every day and submitted a week early if not at the latest 3 days early. 
This minimised the need for willpower by avoiding last second crunches. It also gave me time to chat with classmates who 
were doing it last second and panicking, to correct my own work if I realised I misunderstood a requirement.

For courseworks where I could choose what I would do, I always picked something: fun, practical, novel and I could discuss in
an interview. This set the tipping point of my discipline/willpower tipping point higher for these pieces of work and maximised my
learning. For my dissertation I went all out: I picked Rust (an untouched language), a Raspberry 
Pi as target platform (and embedded systems I had no experience with) and a DashCam system as the project as I wanted to try out
FFmpeg, apply my recent computer vision learnings and make something practical for my mum to use in her car. This ended up 
being one of my best pieces of work.

#### Prioritisation
I've learned on my placement year that wise prioritisation is key to maximising business relevant delivery. 

In university this can be even easier with presence of mark schemes. If a mark scheme awards 10 marks for a pretty
frontend and 10 for automated tests. At work where people maintain code, high quality tests are essential. If it is a small 
throw away uni project, I will always choose prettiness in less time and write tests last if time allows. 

A criteria like "pretty" can be subjective and relative to other students work, if I know my peers are poor at UI then the 
bar might not be that high, I don't need to spend that much time perfecting UI responsiveness or spacing consistency.

My first team project at uni broke me morally, in a team of 5 people, the team lead gave up, 
one wrote a report that scored 99% on a ChatGPT detector. I ended up doing 80% of the coding and picking up where people went 
off radar for weeks and another person broke a database for the demo. 

Watching just a few videos on YouTube on how to lead software development teams, creating healthy team dynamics and 
navigating difficult situations can work wonders. I recommend 
[Healthy Developer on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@HealthyDev). 

For my 2nd team project having learned from 1st project
mistakes and studying Dev Leadership. I was able to confidently take point - we scored 98% for that module, no one was burnt 
out, everyone had autonomy and even extra time to include some bonus features for fun. 

#### Conclusion and Luck
You have probably gathered some of these things might not be feasible for you to apply and your circumstances might be more
or less lucky. I have had the same conclusion from observing many others top performers too. The key is knowing oneself and 
experimenting to find what works rather than copying a blueprint. 

Managing the discipline/willpower tipping line and treating willpower like gold prevented me from burn out while performing.

In fact it was not my plan to graduate at the top of my class, when I received the prize I was surprised. My plan was to 
minimise my chance of not getting a job at the end. But these things and luck just happened to add up to lead me to the top 
of my class as a side effect.

Hopefully you found something useful from this, if you see me in person tell me what works for you and what differs :)
